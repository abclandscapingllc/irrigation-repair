Gilbert Retail Center Landscaping: A Quick Walk-Through Audit for Property Managers

By ABC Landscaping LLC · Mesa, Arizona

Most people who visit a shopping center couldn't tell you what the landscaping looked like. Ask them whether the place felt clean and cared for, though, and they'll answer right away. That gut feeling comes from small things: a parking island full of weeds, a brown shrub next to the pharmacy door, a monument sign you can barely read through the branches.

Tenants pick up on the same signals, and they're paying attention for different reasons. A tidy property tells them the landlord is on top of things. A tired one makes them wonder what else is being ignored.

The good news is that you don't need to hire anyone to find out where your center stands. A phone, a notepad, and about 15 minutes on a weekday morning will do it. This walk-through splits the property into four zones and gives you a short list of things to look for in each. When you're done, you'll know exactly what your retail center landscaping needs, and if you already work with a team that handles commercial landscaping in Gilbert, AZ, you'll walk into your next meeting with real notes instead of a vague sense that "things look a little rough."

Before You Start: A Few Ground Rules

A little prep makes the audit far more useful.

  • Go early. Between 7 and 9 a.m. is ideal. The light is good for photos, the lot isn't packed, and if sprinklers are running you'll actually see them in action.
  • Take photos of everything you flag. Pictures are much harder to argue with than descriptions, and they give you a before-and-after record.
  • Walk it like a customer, not a manager. Park where shoppers park. Walk where they walk. You'll notice things you've stopped seeing from the office.
  • Keep a simple tally. Every item you can't check off counts as one issue. You'll use that number at the end.

Zone 1: First Impressions From the Street About 3 min

Start across the road or at the main entrance drive. Picture yourself driving by at 40 miles an hour and ask what stands out.

  • Can you read every tenant name on the monument sign, or are shrubs and tree limbs creeping over it?
  • Do the street-facing beds look full and healthy, or are there bare spots and dead plants?
  • Is the decomposed granite or rock covering even, or has it thinned out and exposed dirt?
  • Is trash collecting along the curb line or tangled in the plants near the sidewalk?

Why this zone matters: Plenty of Gilbert retail centers sit on busy roads like Gilbert Road, Val Vista, and Williams Field. Thousands of drivers pass every day. For your tenants, a clean and visible street edge is basically a free billboard. A blocked sign or a sad-looking frontage works against them every single hour.

Zone 2: The Parking Lot About 4 min

Now walk the drive aisles and take a close look at the islands, medians, and edges.

  • Are parking islands free of weeds and volunteer grass?
  • Are any shrubs or groundcovers dead, dying, or scorched, especially in islands surrounded by dark asphalt?
  • Do tree canopies clear cars, light poles, and delivery trucks, or are low limbs getting scraped?
  • Do you see wet pavement, puddles, mineral stains, or overspray marks that suggest sprinklers are watering the asphalt instead of the plants?
  • Are there dry, brown patches sitting in the middle of beds that otherwise look fine?

Why this zone matters: Parking islands take more abuse than any other planting area on a retail site. They bake in reflected heat, get clipped by bumpers, and get walked through by shoppers cutting corners. They also hide irrigation problems really well. A broken emitter or a cracked lateral line can go unnoticed for weeks until plants start dying in a strange patch, or until someone notices a water bill that jumped for no clear reason.

If you spot overspray, standing water, or dry patches in an otherwise healthy bed, those are classic signs of a system that needs attention. Small fixes caught early cost far less than replacing a whole island of dead plants in July, which is why many property managers set up regular checks or bring in a crew that offers irrigation repair for Gilbert commercial properties before problems pile up.

Zone 3: Storefronts and Sidewalks About 4 min

Walk the full length of the shops along the sidewalk, the way a customer would on their way from car to door.

  • Are shrubs and plants cut back from walkways, entrances, and door swings?
  • Are entry planters clean, healthy, and free of cigarette butts and cups?
  • Are tree roots pushing up pavers or sidewalk slabs anywhere?
  • Is anything blocking storefront windows, blade signs, or tenant signage?
  • If your center uses seasonal color, is it planted and looking fresh near the main entries?

Why this zone matters: This is where tenants spend their day, so this is where they form their opinion of your management. It's also the area with the most safety exposure. Overgrown shrubs that brush against shoppers, lifted pavement, and slippery spots from leaking planters can all turn into complaints or worse.

Zone 4: Behind the Buildings About 4 min

Finish by walking the rear of the property. Shoppers rarely go back here, but tenants, employees, and delivery drivers are there every day.

  • Are loading zones and service areas clear of trash, debris, and weeds?
  • Are the landscape buffers along block walls and neighboring lots being maintained?
  • Are trees close to the building trimmed back from rooflines and gutters?
  • Are drainage channels, scuppers, and retention areas free of trash and plant buildup?

Why this zone matters: Problems often start in the back because nobody looks there. Leaks, pest activity, and clogged drainage tend to show up behind buildings first. And when a tenant takes a delivery next to a weed-choked wall, they notice. They may not say anything, but they remember it when lease renewal comes around.

Scoring Your Walk-Through

Add up the items you couldn't check off across all four zones.

Issues FoundWhat It Usually Means
0 to 2Your property is in good shape. Keep the current routine going and repeat this audit monthly.
3 to 4Routine maintenance is slipping in a few spots. Share your photos with your landscaper and ask for a targeted cleanup.
5 or moreSomething bigger is going on, particularly if most issues are in Zone 2. It often points to irrigation trouble or a service schedule that isn't keeping up with the size and use of the property.

Pay attention to patterns, too. If almost every issue sits in one zone, that's where your service plan needs the most work.

A Quick Word on Gilbert's Seasons

Timing matters in the East Valley. Running this audit once a month is a good habit, but a few points in the year are worth circling on the calendar:

  • Late spring (April to May): Check irrigation coverage before the real heat sets in. Weak spots now become dead plants by June.
  • Mid-summer (July to August): Look for heat stress in parking islands and around west-facing walls.
  • After monsoon storms: Walk the property for broken limbs, washed-out rock, and debris in drainage areas.
  • Early fall (October): A good window for replanting, refreshing seasonal color, and catching up on pruning before the holiday shopping rush.

Audits catch problems, but your service schedule decides how often they come back. Frequently Asked Questions

How often should I audit my retail center's landscaping?

Once a month works well for most centers. Add a quick walk after any major storm.

Should I share my audit results with my landscaping company?

Yes. Photos and a simple list make it easy for your provider to understand what you're seeing and fix it quickly. A good crew will welcome the input.

What's the most common landscaping problem on Gilbert retail properties?

Irrigation issues in parking islands come up again and again. Heat, wear, and hard-to-see leaks make those areas the first to struggle.
